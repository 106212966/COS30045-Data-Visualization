Storyboard 2: The TNB Boss Battle

How much does sizing up actually cost you? Let's check the stats of the Small, Medium, and Large categories.

The Setup

To analyze the financial impact of larger screens, we grouped the televisions into three distinct tiers: Small (<44 inches), Medium (44 to 65 inches), and Large (>65 inches).

The Conflict

While consumers may be tempted to upgrade to the 'Large' category for a wider viewing angle, they often overlook the recurring financial impact on their monthly electricity utility bills.

The Investigation

We calculated the Average Labelled Energy Consumption (kWh/year) for each specific tier to quantify the exact difference in power usage across categories.

The Reveal

The data shows that upgrading from the Small to the Medium category results in a moderate increase in energy use. However, crossing into the Large category triggers a severe spike in power consumption.

The Insight

Television energy consumption scales aggressively with size. Pushing past 65 inches exponentially increases the surface area that requires illumination, significantly driving up utility costs.

The Verdict

The 'Medium' category (44 to 65 inches) represents the optimal balance. It delivers a substantial viewing experience while maintaining a reasonable and manageable annual energy consumption rate.
