Buying an Energy-Efficient Television

When purchasing a television, the size of the screen is a major factor in both viewing comfort and energy consumption. It is recommended to choose a size that fits your room and viewing distance, rather than simply buying the largest screen available. Once you decide on a size, look for the most energy-efficient model by comparing the Star Ratings and the estimated annual electricity usage (kWh) on the Energy Rating Label.

Display Technologies & Features

Different TV screens use energy differently:

  • LED: The main display technology today, using a liquid crystal display (LCD) with LED backlighting.
  • OLED: Organic light-emitting diodes create light directly without requiring a backlight.

Keep in mind that higher resolutions (like 4K and 8K UHD), larger screens, and internet connectivity will increase energy use. A simple tip to reduce your TV's consumption is to turn down the screen brightness.

Estimated Annual Running Costs

Power consumption varies significantly based on TV type, star rating, size, and your viewing habits. Below is an approximate annual cost comparison (assuming 3 hours of use per day at an energy rate of 27c/kWh for a 90-110cm screen):

TV Type Star Rating Estimated Annual Cost
LCD (LED) 7 star to 2.5 star $9.89 to $30.79
LCD 6 star to 2.5 star $10.31 to $28.96
OLED 5 star to 2.5 star $25.56 to $50.08

Energy-Saving Tips

  • Check the Specs: Look at the sticker on the back of your TV to find its power rating and consumption data.
  • Switch it Off: While convenient to leave on standby, turning your TV off at the wall when not in use is highly recommended to stop passive energy drain.
